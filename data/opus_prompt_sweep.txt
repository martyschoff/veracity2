We are building veracity2 (this repo): a predictions-vs-reality tracker. Predictions are extracted from commentators' shows, gated (prediction/prescription/fact/conditional), timestamped, and then judged. The judgment engine is the "verdict sweep" — scripts/panel_adjudicate.py — which runs weighted LLM panelist votes over a person's predictions: it picks 3 random panelists per prediction, sends claim + transcript excerpt to qwen3:8b endpoints (4-GPU Ollama pool, round-robin via POOL list), tallies correct=+weight / incorrect=-weight / unclear=0 using each panelist's tier weight (panel_weight field: 1.5 professional forecasters / 1.25 specialist analysts / 1.0 commentators+insiders), and sets verdict correct/wrong at |score| >= 2.0.

Known weaknesses we already suspect: single-threaded execution; concurrent runs clobber data/predictions.json (the MiroFish worker and swarm write the same file); random panelist sampling gives no coverage guarantee; votes for not-yet-due predictions produce noisy "incorrect" judgments (the swarm had a future-dated bias we patched in its prompt — panel_adjudicate.py may need the same); no calibration tracking of which panelists are actually accurate; no retry/persistence of partial votes if the run dies mid-sweep (state saves only every 5 predictions); POOL endpoints are hardcoded and a dead instance stalls its share of votes until timeout.

QUESTION: Review scripts/panel_adjudicate.py (and how it reads data/predictions.json and panel weights) and tell us what improvements YOU would make to the verdict sweep, prioritized. Consider: correctness (vote integrity, dedup, retry), speed (parallelism across the pool, sharding without write races), judgment quality (prompt design, panelist selection strategy, calibration, handling future-dated claims), observability (logging, per-panelist accuracy stats), and integration (how the sweep should chain with the MiroFish worker in scripts/miro_worker.py and the swarm in scripts/monte_carlo.py, which share predictions.json).
