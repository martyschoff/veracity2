# Energy Panel Review — October 2026

Veracity2 tracks predictions from commentators and uses LLM panelists to vote on verdicts. This document reviews the 32-member ENERGY panel for cuts, additions, and weighting.

**Evaluation criteria:**
1. **Prediction density** — Do they make dated, testable claims?
2. **Independence** — Are they analysts or pure advocates?
3. **Extractability** — Own-voice shows vs scattered guest appearances?
4. **Overlap/redundancy** — Does someone else already cover this niche?
5. **Ideological balance** — Is the panel skewed?

## WEIGHTS

**Recommendation: Yes, apply weights.**

Flat weights treat Jim Cramer's throwaway stock pick the same as Helima Croft's structured RBC forecast. That's obviously wrong. Weighting should reflect:
- **Expertise depth** — Is this their specialty or a side comment?
- **Methodology** — Do they show their work?
- **Track record** — Do we have historical accuracy data?
- **Skin in the game** — Are they accountable?

### Proposed Weighting Scheme

| Tier | Weight | Criteria | Members |
|------|--------|----------|---------|
| **A: Professional Forecasters** | 1.5× | Primary job is energy forecasting. Institutional backing. Published methodology. | Tom Kloza (OPIS), Helima Croft (RBC), John Kilduff (Again Capital), Pavel Molchanov (Raymond James), Amrita Sen (Energy Aspects), Anas Alhajji, Arjun Murti, Ed Crooks (WoodMac), Michael Lynch |
| **B: Specialist Analysts** | 1.25× | Deep energy expertise. Own-voice content. Track record exists. | Peter Zeihan, Doomberg, Dan Yergin, Nate Hagens, Robert Bryce, Art Berman, David Blackmon, Mark P. Mills, Robert Rapier, Alex Epstein, Mark Nelson, Steven Koonin, Josh Young, Tracy Shuchart, Javier Blas, Jesse Jenkins, Vaclav Smil |
| **C: Industry Insiders** | 1.0× | C-suite/government. Their predictions reflect institutional positioning, not independent analysis. Valuable signal but not forecasting per se. | Chris Wright (DOE), Mike Wirth (Chevron) |
| **D: Fact-Checkers** | 1.0× | Reference sources, not predictors. | FactCheck.org, AP Fact Check, Google Fact Check Explorer |

### How Weights Apply

**Verdict voting:** When LLM panelists vote on whether a prediction was correct:
- A-tier vote counts as 1.5 votes
- B-tier vote counts as 1.25 votes
- C/D-tier vote counts as 1.0 votes

**2-vote rule adjustment:** Current rule is "2 votes → verdict." With weighting:
- 2 A-tier votes = 3.0 → strong verdict
- 1 A-tier + 1 B-tier = 2.75 → verdict
- 2 B-tier votes = 2.5 → verdict
- 2 C-tier votes = 2.0 → weak verdict (consider requiring 2.5 minimum)

**Why not higher weights?**
- Going above 1.5× gives single voices too much power
- The point is *marginal* differentiation, not making A-tier panelists dominate
- Keep weights narrow (1.0–1.5 range) to preserve panel diversity

### Alternative: Binary Expert/Non-Expert

If the tiered system feels overengineered:

| Tier | Weight | Members |
|------|--------|---------|
| Expert | 1.25× | Anyone whose primary job is energy analysis |
| Standard | 1.0× | Everyone else |

This is simpler but loses the distinction between professional forecasters (paid to be right) and specialist analysts (paid to have opinions).
